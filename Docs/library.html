The Two-Gate 5% EV Filter Explained

Published 2026-03-15  ·  SureBet Archer Engine v1.0

Every pick on ArcVest passes through two independent filters before it's logged. Most candidates fail. The ones that survive are genuine edges — not just low odds or high-probability favorites.

Gate 1: Expected Value ≥ 5%

Expected value (EV) measures how much you expect to profit per dollar risked, on average, if you made the same bet thousands of times.

EV = (model_win_probability × decimal_odds) − 1 Example: Model says 60% win probability. Book offers 2.00 odds. EV = (0.60 × 2.00) − 1 = 0.20 = 20% EV ✓ Passes Gate 1

The model win probability comes from Pythagorean expectation — a formula that uses points scored and points allowed to estimate true team strength, independently of the win-loss record.

Why 5%? A 5% EV threshold filters out noise while capturing real edge. At high volume, even 5-10% EV picks are profitable over hundreds of bets. Lower thresholds produce too many marginal picks; higher thresholds produce too few.

Gate 2: Positive Edge vs. No-Vig Probability

Even a pick with positive EV might just be exploiting overpriced odds — not a genuine model edge. The second gate compares the model probability directly against the no-vig (fair) implied probability.

No-vig probability = (1/odds_side1) / (1/odds_side1 + 1/odds_side2) Edge = model_probability − no_vig_probability A pick must have Edge > 0 to pass Gate 2.

This ensures the model actually disagrees with the market's fair probability — not just the vig-inflated line. A pick can have positive EV but negative edge if the book's fair odds are stronger than the model. That pick gets filtered out.

Why Both Gates?

EV alone can be gamed by betting huge underdogs — technically positive EV but high variance. Edge alone doesn't account for the actual profit magnitude. Together, they ensure every logged pick is both mathematically profitable in expectation and based on genuine model disagreement with the market.

In practice: On a typical day, several hundred raw candidates enter the pipeline. After the two-gate filter, 40-80 survive. Those are the picks. Every single one cleared both bars independently.

What Comes Next: Kelly Sizing

After filtering, each pick receives a quarter Kelly stake — a conservative position size derived from the EV and odds. All picks are then scaled uniformly so total daily risk stays within 10% of bankroll. No single bet dominates the portfolio. That's the final step before publication.
